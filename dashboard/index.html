The value model

A size curve through the traded set, family offsets on top of it, and nothing else the gate did not pass.

For every traded protocol the snapshot records a market cap and a TVL. fitStaged (lib/model.ts) first fits the size curve by least squares in log space: log10 value = — + — · log10 TVL over the — traded protocols with a positive cap and TVL. A slope under 1 is the snapshot's own statement that value grows slower than TVL; it is also what reorders the board's top, where a flat multiple simply sorts by TVL. Each category's family offset is then the median of its members' curve residuals, shrunk toward its parent family's offset with weight n/(n+8); the parent offset is the pooled median residual of the parent's categories, shrunk toward the curve with weight n/(n+6). A family with fewer than 3 traded members gets no offset, and its protocols price on the curve — the row's basis column says so. No protocol name is hardcoded anywhere in the fit.

Tokenized and pre-token protocols are never mixed — the board holds only protocols that record no token (market cap, gecko id and symbol all null), and it draws the same product-type line the watchlist definition draws: wrapped, staked or bridged products of a token that already trades are not launches waiting to happen, so bridge, liquid-staking and restaking product categories, exchange-fronted names and version-suffixed names sit aside (— protocols, counted in Data quality). The board applies that line without the watchlist's $50M floor — the floor is a size line, the product-type line is a kind line, and only the kind line is kept.

Farm economics — how every plan is costed, and what Scenario S assumes

Every plan is priced in integer cents from its own protocol's inputs and closed by a verdict; Scenario S is an assumption set, printed in full — not a forecast.

Each plan is built by buildPlan from the protocol's mechanism — the steps name what that mechanism actually does (supply-borrow-repay cycles for a lending loop, crossings in both directions for a bridge, epochs for an options vault, completed terms for a locker) — and priced in integer cents, so a plan's step costs sum to its stated total exactly. The plan's shape: a working position from the protocol's TVL band (bands run $50–$300 up to $2,500–$10,000; the modeled position is the band midpoint), a window of 5–10 weeks by band, two sessions a week, and modeled time of 45 minutes of setup plus one hour a session. Gas is charged per session from a stated schedule — Ethereum $4.50, Solana $0.06, BNB Chain $0.25, Tron $0.30, layer-2 chains $0.35, any other chain $0.60 — split as 2 sessions of setup, 1 to open, 1 to breadth and the rest to the core cadence. Protocol fees come from the mechanism's own formula, printed in each detail view: borrow interest at a modeled 6% APR on a 55% loan-to-value loop for lending, taker fees at 0.05% on 3× notional for perps, premiums at 2% of position per weekly roll for options, relayer fees at 0.1% of the amount crossed plus $0.75 a crossing for bridges, and so on. Mechanisms whose honest cost is gas alone — vault deposits, staking, governance — are charged gas alone; the model does not invent fees to look thorough.

Scenario S is the reward side, and it is an assumption set, not a forecast: the protocol launches a token at exactly this model's implied value; 5% of that value goes to early users; and the pool is shared by capital pro-rata over an assumed farmed 25% of TVL (not all TVL farms). A plan's Scenario S reward is therefore 0.05 ÷ 0.25 × its protocol's effective multiple (implied value ÷ TVL) × its modeled position — which is why two plans share economics only when their inputs genuinely coincide. Every plan prints the reward three ways: the net, the net per hour of modeled work, and the break-even share — the percentage of the Scenario S reward the plan needs to receive to cover its cost. The verdict keys on the deposit model: FARM when the reward covers the cost twice over (break-even at or under 50%), THIN when it covers the cost but by less, SKIP when it does not cover the cost at all. Beside Scenario S, every plan also prints the pure pro-rata bound — the same 5% pool shared over all of TVL — which is the harshest simple reading. What the costing leaves out, stated once: perps funding (it cuts both ways), LP impermanent loss, yield a position earns while it sits (supply yield, pool fees — real, and excluded, which makes these nets conservative), creator royalties, and any value of the farmer's hours beyond the hours themselves, which are printed. A protocol is only ever said to run a points programme conditionally — check its official channels; the snapshot cannot know.

Implied values inherit every limit of the fitted model: the out-of-fold factor band printed beside each row is the honest size of that blindness, and Scenario S inherits the same error twice over, since its reward is proportional to the implied value — treat every net on this board as a planning figure with a wide interval around it, not as a quote.
